# Mentor Guide

## Role of the adult

The adult is not primarily a lecturer. The useful role is to ask short questions that make Mikey predict, notice, compare, and explain.

## Prompt pattern

Before an experiment:

> What do you think will happen?

After it runs:

> What happened?

Then:

> Why do you think the computer did that?

Finally:

> What could we change to test that idea?

## Jackpot example

Useful questions include:

- Why does the light look like it is moving?
- Is one LED traveling, or are different LEDs turning on?
- What will happen if we make the wheel faster?
- What input tells the computer to stop?
- What must the computer compare to decide whether it is a jackpot?
- What is different between RUNNING and STOPPED?

## Avoid turning play into a test

Not every round needs a question. PLAY mode should remain genuinely fun. Use LEARN and CHALLENGE when Mikey is interested and engaged.

## Vocabulary

Introduce a technical word after the experience whenever possible.

Example sequence:

1. Mikey sees that the machine can be spinning or stopped.
2. Discuss that the machine needs to remember which situation it is in.
3. Introduce the word **state**.

## Debugging conversations

When something is intentionally or genuinely wrong, avoid immediately giving the fix.

Ask:

- What did you expect?
- What did you observe?
- What is one thing that might cause the difference?
- What single test could help us find out?

## IDE transition

When Mikey is ready for source code, begin with code that controls a behavior he already knows.

Prefer:

> This number is the wheel speed you have been changing in Settings.

before:

> Today we are learning integer variables.

The familiar behavior gives the syntax meaning.
